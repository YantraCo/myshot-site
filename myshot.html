How it works

Capture · Annotate · Share
01 Capture MyShot draws its own selector on every platform — an 8× magnifier showing true pixels, crosshair guides, a live dimension readout, and arrow-key nudging down to a single physical pixel. Region, full screen, or the active window. Works on Wayland, where most tools hand you the desktop's own picker
02 Annotate Arrows, boxes, circles, text, freehand, highlighter, numbered counters, crop and blur — with full undo across every one of them. Blur genuinely destroys what's underneath, so a redaction survives being shared, zoomed and re-saved. Optional — skip straight to sharing if the raw capture is enough
03 Share The file uploads to your Drive, filed by type and month, and a link lands on the clipboard. Paste it into Slack or GitHub and the image renders inline rather than showing a dead link card. Offline or signed out, captures queue and upload later — nothing is lost

Details that took the longest

Four of them
01

Pixel-exact on fractional scaling

On a 125%-scaled display, the toolkit reports a device pixel ratio of 2.0 while the true scale is 1.25. Trusting it puts every crop out by 60%. MyShot derives scale from the captured image itself, so the region you drew is the region you get.

02

A real selector on Wayland

Wayland forbids apps from reading the screen, so most tools surrender and show the desktop's own screenshot UI. MyShot asks the system for pixels silently, then draws its own selector over them — same magnifier, same crosshairs, same shortcuts as everywhere else.

03

A capture is never lost

If the upload fails — no network, expired session, Drive having a bad day — the capture stays queued on disk with its retry schedule and drains when things recover. Failure is designed for, not discovered later.

04

You decide what "shared" means

Before the very first upload MyShot asks whether links should be public or private, and explains the trade-off in plain words. Screenshots routinely catch passwords, tokens and customer data; that isn't a default anyone should set on your behalf.

On privacy

The short version

MyShot has no accounts and no servers. Your captures live on your computer and, if you connect Drive, in your own Google account. We never receive them, and there is nothing for us to leak.

A public link means anyone holding the URL can view that file — not listed, not searchable, but not private either. MyShot asks you to choose before the first upload instead of deciding for you. The full detail is in the privacy policy.

Status

In development

Capture, Drive upload and sharing work today. The annotation editor, system tray and global hotkeys are being built now. MyShot is not yet generally available.
